About Me

A designer who owns the product, not just the screens

I have around 4 years in product design, the last two of them embedded at Listen2It, a text-to-speech and audio platform. I now split my time between UI/UX design and AI product management — writing the brief, designing against it, and seeing it through to release.

Most of my work sits in a category that didn't have patterns yet: real-time avatar agents, voice studios, text-to-video. There is no library to borrow from, so the job is figuring out what the interface should even be — where an agent should live on screen, when to ask for a permission, what a user needs to see before they trust the output.

I build as well as design. Figma for the thinking, Cursor and Claude Code for turning it into something clickable. Getting to a working prototype quickly means decisions get settled by using the thing rather than arguing about a static frame.

Design System

Listen2It — Marketing Site & Page System

Product Designer · Listen2It · 2025–26

Redesigned Listen2It's marketing site end to end — homepage, pricing, and the full set of feature pages — then turned it into a system the team could extend without me. The design language is deliberately split: a bold, saturated homepage, and quieter, minimal inner pages that let the product do the talking. The hero had to carry a widened story too, from text-to-speech alone to voiceover, dubbing, music and sound effects in one studio.

The harder half was scale. A catalogue of 1,400+ voices across 84 base languages needs a page per language, so I designed one template that holds up across roughly 155 of them, plus use-case landing pages for presentation voiceover, video voiceover and audiobook narration. Components were drawn to migrate cleanly into Strapi as the site moved off Webflow.

Listen2It Dashboard — AI Voice Studio

Product Designer · Listen2It

Design work across the app itself: the multi-track studio, the composer workflow, voice cloning and voiceover generation. The dashboard homepage was the main shift — Voice Cloning, AI Voiceovers and Generate Music were real capabilities buried behind navigation, so I pulled them up as first-class entry points people land on rather than go looking for.

I also ran QA on the staging build after the redesign, covering generation, composer, cloning and export paths, and wrote the structured test prompts the team now reuses for those passes. On the embed side, I compared BotTalk, BeyondWords, Trinity Audio and ElevenLabs Audio Native and argued for a Web Component with Shadow DOM over an iframe, so a publisher's player inherits their styling without leaking scope either direction.

UX Strategy

Avatario — Real-Time AI Avatar Agent

UX Strategy · Avatario

Avatario puts a live avatar agent, Aria, into your product. Onboarding had a structural problem: Aria floats on top of the interface she is explaining, so the guidance and the thing being explained compete for the same pixels. Users either dismissed her or lost the screen underneath.

I restructured it into three layers — an intent-capture screen before the workspace loads, a persistent sidebar rail that carries progress out of the overlay, and Aria repositioned as support on demand rather than a guide you have to sit through. I made a similar call on Chrome's screen-share prompt, which was firing before anyone knew why it was needed; it became an inline button at the moment of use. I'm now designing agent templates for the Create Agent flow.

AI Video

Voomo — AI Video Generation Platform

Product Designer · Voomo

Voomo turns a script into video with AI avatars and voice, and translates it across 145+ languages. I worked on the generation surfaces — picking an avatar, choosing a voice, getting from text to a first usable cut without the interface demanding that you already understand how any of it works.

Alongside the UI I helped think through Voomo Publisher's pitch: whether auto-generating video for long-tail articles actually pays for itself on pre-roll advertising. The arithmetic set a real ceiling on what the product could promise publishers, and it changed how we framed and priced it.
